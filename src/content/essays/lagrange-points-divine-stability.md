Lagrange points are locations in space where gravitational forces balance perfectly. At these points, an object can remain stable with minimal effort—the competing pulls of massive bodies cancel out, creating zones of equilibrium.

The most useful is L2—a million miles from Earth on the side away from the sun. Several telescopes orbit there because it's stable, dark, and maintains consistent position relative to Earth and sun. It's a place of rest amid powerful forces.

This provides a picture of spiritual rest—finding stability amid competing demands, discovering equilibrium amid life's gravitational pulls, resting in God while cosmic forces swirl around us.

## The Physics

Lagrange points exist where gravitational attraction and centrifugal force balance. L1 sits between Earth and sun—pulled equally by both. L2 is beyond Earth from the sun. L3 is opposite Earth on far side of solar orbit. L4 and L5 trail and lead Earth in its orbit.

These aren't physical locations—they're geometric points where forces balance. Objects placed there require minimal energy to maintain position. They're gravitationally stable—small perturbations don't send them spiraling away.

## Competing Forces

Life involves competing forces pulling different directions. Work demands time. Family needs attention. Ministry requires energy. Self-care fights for space. Financial pressures, social obligations, physical limitations—all exert gravitational pull.

These aren't necessarily evil forces. They're legitimate demands creating real tension. The question isn't eliminating forces but finding balance points where we can maintain stability amid them.

## My Autistic Energy Budget

As an autistic person, I feel these competing forces intensely. Social interaction drains energy. Sensory overload depletes reserves. Executive function demands are exhausting. I have limited capacity facing unlimited demands.

Finding Lagrange points is survival necessity, not luxury. I need equilibrium positions where forces balance—where I can rest without being pulled apart by competing gravitational wells.

## Divine Gravity

Jesus offers rest: "Come to me, all who are weary and burdened, and I will give you rest" (Matthew 11:28). This isn't eliminating life's demands. It's finding equilibrium point where divine gravity balances earthly pulls.

God's gravity is stronger than competing forces. Like the sun outweighing Earth, divine presence can counterbalance accumulated earthly demands. The Lagrange point isn't escaping forces—it's positioning where they balance.

## Sabbath as L2

Sabbath is weekly Lagrange point—time when competing demands balance through deliberate rest. Work pulls one direction, but Sabbath command pulls opposite direction with equal force.

This isn't natural equilibrium—it's commanded balance. God established weekly rest precisely because natural forces would otherwise pull us into constant activity. Sabbath creates artificial but necessary Lagrange point.

## Minimal Station-Keeping

Objects at Lagrange points require minimal station-keeping—small adjustments to maintain position. They're stable but not static. External perturbations (solar radiation pressure, gravitational influences from other bodies) require occasional correction.

Spiritual rest similarly requires maintenance. Finding equilibrium doesn't mean permanent autopilot. We need regular adjustment—continued prayer, renewed commitment, ongoing discipline. Rest is sustained through small corrections, not achieved once and forgotten.

## The Halo Orbit

Satellites at L2 don't sit exactly at the point—they orbit around it in "halo orbits." The actual point is mathematically unstable (solar radiation pressure pushes objects away), so spacecraft orbit nearby in stable loops.

Perhaps perfect rest is similarly theoretical. We don't achieve perfect equilibrium but orbit around it—coming close, drifting slightly, returning through correction. The goal isn't mathematical perfection but practical stability.

## American Constant Motion

American culture resists rest. Productivity is virtue. Busyness is badge of honor. Rest feels like laziness. We're pulled by achievement gravity, accomplishment demands, success pressures.

Lagrange points challenge this. Stability amid forces isn't weakness—it's physics. Rest isn't unproductive—it's necessary. Balance points enable sustained operation that constant acceleration destroys.

## Temporary Equilibrium

Lagrange points exist because gravitational configuration creates them. Change the configuration (planets move, masses shift), and equilibrium points move.

Our rest points similarly shift as life circumstances change. What worked in one season might not work in another. Job changes, family grows, health fluctuates—the gravitational landscape changes, requiring new balance points.

This isn't failure—it's adaptation. Finding rest is ongoing process, not one-time achievement.

## Conclusion

Lagrange points are zones where gravitational forces balance, enabling stability with minimal energy expense. They're not escape from forces but positions where forces equilibrate.

Rest in God works similarly. Not escaping life's demands but finding positions where divine presence balances earthly pulls. Not eliminating forces but discovering equilibrium amid them.
